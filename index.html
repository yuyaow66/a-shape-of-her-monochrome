WHO SHAPES HER LIFE?

How social expectations affect Chinese women’s life

A Shape of Her is a personal research project in which I look at how social expectations affect the lives of Chinese women. It starts with words that a girl may tell herself many times as she matures; “I should behave,” “When are you getting married?” and “What will happen to your job once you have children?” When you hear those words, they are sometimes in a place of concern; but I think they also have ideas of how a woman should be. I would like to know how these ideas become a part of her and if the life she desires is what she wants or the life she has learned to want.

In this project I would use interviews with women around me as case studies, including my mother, grandmother, and aunts. They are all at different stages of life and have different experiences. I was seeking them out in my daily life, what matters to them and if you could go back in time and change one thing about them what would it be? I am very close to them but I realize there are still things I don’t know about them. I know my mother as my mother, but I would love to know who she was before she had me, what she wanted to do, and how she feels about her life now.

And I learned from those conversations that being happy with life didn’t mean not having regrets. My grandmother might be happy now but she wishes she had not married or left the village where she used to live. And women can enjoy taking care of their family but also wish for more time off for themselves. These feelings do not balance out my reality. I want to understand what she means when she talks about regret and not necessarily try to help her feel better or say to her that she shouldn’t think like that.

And I also study Chinese women in movies and literature beyond the interviews. I see the characters and relate them to women I know. I look at how a woman goes about being a good daughter, wife, or mother in the eyes of others and what is going on in her own mind in this process. Sometimes she remains silent because she’s worried about what people could think. Sometimes a decision appears to be her own, but she doesn’t have many other choices. Reading these stories makes me think about the interviews differently. The interviews also help me see things in the films and books that I hadn’t realized before.

I put these interviews and my reading notes on the website with family pictures and words I collected from everyday life. The paper slips contain questions and remarks about what is expected of Chinese women. One word by itself might be ordinary or even harmless. But together I can see how these expectations are built up. A girl might be encouraged to work hard and be independent but then is expected to put her family before her work. I want to know how she feels when she cannot meet both expectations and if she thinks she has to pick something for herself because it’s going to hurt someone else.

The expectations change as she gets older. Quiet and obedient behavior is probably viewed as OK as a child. Later on people might be more interested in her appearance or marriage and children. Looking after others can be a huge part of her life as well and the things she wants to do might have to wait. But I don’t think every woman is faced with this in the same way. In case she is able to choose, it is based on her financial situation and support from those around her. And I want to know what caring for her family means to her personally too, both values that make her tired and does not represent all Chinese women as the women I interview.

My empathy towards people I know is based on how I am trying to get to know them more, I’m trying to know them better. I don’t expect their answers to be the same, or one way to describe how women feel. I hope through making A Shape of Her to pay attention to what a woman wants for herself, even if it’s different from what other people want for her. I think that understanding this starts with asking her and letting her finish her answer, even if it’s not what I thought she would say.
